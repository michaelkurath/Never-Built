# Boeing 2707 promotion — 7 October 2026

Exhibit 004; reviewed editorial score 24/25 (4, 5, 5, 5, 5).

## Source and text review

- [The Museum of Flight](https://blog.museumofflight.org/seattles-supersonic-legacy)
  confirms December 1966 selection, the 1971 withdrawal of federal funding,
  the absence of a testable flight prototype, and the surviving 2707-200 mockup
  nose. High costs and sonic-boom opposition influenced withdrawal; the display
  gives the documented funding decision without claiming a single cause.
- [NASA TM-109089](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19940021652/downloads/19940021652.pdf),
  pp. 7–8 and Figure 23, distinguishes the swing-wing 2707-200 from the later
  fixed-wing 2707-300. The former candidate's Boeing PDF URL returned 404 and
  was replaced with the accessible museum account.
- [Museum archive](https://archives.museumofflight.org/repositories/2/resources/553)
  confirms surviving cutaway drawings of the unrealised SST.

1966 is the contract-selection year, not the first design work. The artwork
depicts the later fixed-wing design; the note distinguishes the museum's
earlier mockup. Display text passed character limits. The entry remains an
artistic reconstruction, never presented as a photograph or engineering plan.

tailplanes and four underwing nacelles against the NASA drawing. The first
generation's wing was too generic; the final edit added the forward strakes
and leading-edge kinks. Both grayscale exports preserve the entire silhouette.

quadrant on OG (800 × 480, 1-bit), X landscape (1040 × 780, 4-bit) and X portrait
(780 × 1040, 4-bit). The OG quadrant title/caption overlap was corrected by
grouping its text and reserving a supported fixed image height. No title,
caption or aircraft silhouette is clipped.
