Promoted Tatlin’s Tower (008, 22/25) and OWL 100-metre Telescope (009, 21/25).

## Source and text review

- Tatlin: Centre Pompidou confirms the unbuilt full monument, the surviving documentation and its 1979 reconstructed model. Museum Tinguely’s 2012 retrospective supplies 1919–1920 and the contributing barriers: civil war, scarce resources and technical limits. The reconstructed model is not the original and no single cancellation date is invented.
- OWL: ESO’s road-to-ELT history dates the completed study to 1998–2005 and records the recommendation for a smaller, less risky telescope. Archived 2004 design material confirms a segmented optical primary, smaller secondary, open steel structure and rail-mounted alt-azimuth concept. OWL is distinct from the 39-metre ELT.
- L-2000: Smithsonian’s search-visible collection record confirms loss to Boeing in 1966; full page retrieval returned 403. NASA’s Contributions to Aeronautics, volume 2, pp. 591–592 was retrieved and read independently. The 1966 competition decision is separate from the 1971 SST programme cancellation. The candidate retains pending review for early dates and exact mock-up configuration.
- Joint Core System: MoMA’s fully retrieved record explicitly dates Isozaki’s unrealised Shinjuku elevation to 1960. Keep this specific design separate from later City in the Air imagery. No invented cancellation, client or funding failure.

## Artwork

Both new images use original built-in Imagegen black-ink engravings on white paper.
Tatlin: inclined tapering spiral trusses around a leaning spine, suspended cube, pyramid, cylinder and upper curved volume. Museum reconstructions differ; this is an interpretation rather than an exact replica.
OWL: broad shallow segmented optical primary, smaller secondary and open support steelwork. The drawing preserves identifying optical-telescope features; exact segment counts, truss joints and perspective are illustrative.
