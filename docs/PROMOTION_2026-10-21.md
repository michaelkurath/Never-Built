## Source and fit review

The Glass House and Queens Museum place Fuller’s 1960 Dome over Manhattan in Never Built New York’s unrealised-project context. The Bureau International des Expositions separately describes the Manhattan environmental-control proposal and Fuller’s built Expo 1967 Montreal pavilion: https://theglasshouse.org/whats-on/never-built-new-york/ and https://bureauinternationaldesexpositions.bie-paris.org/site/en/latest/infocus/entry/world-expos-and-architectonic-structures-an-intimate-relationship . The montage, drawings and later exhibition visualisations are not a completed enclosure or functioning prototype; Montreal’s pavilion was a separate project.

New candidate Broadacre City: MoMA identifies the decentralised city as a proposal developed from the 1930s, while Columbia’s Frank Lloyd Wright archive inventory explicitly labels the master plan, model and component studies as unbuilt projects: https://www.moma.org/collection/works/161773 and https://www1.columbia.edu/sec/cu/libraries/inside/avery_assets/FLWFA_DWGS_LOCATION.pdf . The surviving 12-foot model is presentation material, not a completed city. Later buildings influenced by individual ideas do not amount to implementation of the master plan. Rating: recognition 4, visual strength 5, story strength 5, evidence clarity 5, catalogue fit 5 = 24/25.
